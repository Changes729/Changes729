# Unleashing Social Sector Creativity with Design

# 通过设计解放社会领域的创造力

Making effective use of the creative power of design requires engaging entire organizations—from leadership to the front lines.

要想有效运用设计的创造力，需要动员整个组织——从领导层到一线员工。



Innovations that truly make leaps are not easy. They require that organizations reorient their work so that it focuses on their overall impact goal, not just the individual beneficiary at the center of the innovation project. As a result, design-thinking methods, such as human-centered design, are helpful but not sufficient. Organizations need to find ways to link innovation projects directly to their system change and transformative impact goals, and get everyone involved—from the executives to the front lines.

真正能实现飞跃的创新并不容易。它要求组织重新调整他们的工作方向，使工作聚焦到整体影响目标，而非仅关注创新项目核心的个体受益者。因此，诸如以人为本的设计思维等方法虽有帮助，却远远不够。组织需寻找将创新项目直接与系统变革及转型影响目标相联结的方式，并让所有人参与其中——从高管到一线员工。



**Activism-driven innovation**

**行动主义驱动的创新**



Failure to center innovation on impact can derail it. For example, the workforce development organization [Year Up](http://www.yearup.org/), where I was an innovation advisor, is focused on making its model more shareable so that other organizations could take advantage of its evidence-based, high-impact model. But our plan to run micro-trials hit some barriers. Year Up’s frontline service providers were focused on providing the best service to the program’s individual beneficiaries and were concerned about changing how they worked (for example, trying a blended learning instead of a traditional classroom approach). It required convincing of the frontline staff, because the organization had not yet made the connection between the value proposition for the direct-service programs and the value proposition for growing transformative impact across the organization. From a human-centered perspective, we were aligned in wanting to do what was best for beneficiaries, but we needed to cultivate buy-in from the frontline workers to use system change as the way to grow impact and serve more people. Organizations need to find ways to take the very powerful idea of human-centered design, and develop it into a broader “activism driven” stakeholder and impact-centric approach.

若未能将创新聚焦于影响本身，就有可能导致创新脱离正常的轨道。举个例子，我曾担任创新顾问的劳动力发展组织[Year Up](http://www.yearup.org/)，致力于使其模式更易于推广，以便其他机构能借鉴其基于实证的高影响力模式。但我们进行微型试验的计划遭遇了阻碍。Year Up的一线服务人员专注于为项目受益者提供最佳服务，对改变工作方式（例如尝试混合式学习替代传统课堂教学）心存顾虑。由于该组织尚未在直接服务项目与全组织范围内产生的变革性影响力增长之间建立起价值主张（value proposition）之间的关联，因此需要说服一线员工。从以人为本的视角出发，我们一致致力于为受益者创造最佳的体验，但需要培养一线员工对系统变革的认同——唯有通过系统变革才能扩大影响力并服务更多人群。组织需要探索如何将极具力量的以人为本设计理念，发展为更广泛的“行动主义驱动（activism driven）”的利益相关方与影响力导向策略（impact-centric approach）。



**Handing over the authorship of micro-trials to the community**

**将微型试验的著作权移交给社区**



The need to get everyone involved doesn’t stop at the prototyping or “making” phase of design thinking. Prototyping comes out of the idea of experiments, where a researcher or designer goes in and tests something, then interprets the results. However, that process doesn’t work as well in the social sector, given the sector’s complex ecosystems, wide range of stakeholders and variables, and sensitivity to both perceived and actual “do no harm” requirements. Doing a single-variable probe (such as you would attempt in a randomized control trial) as a test to see what happens is too simplistic. The idea—the prototype—needs to live as part of the community, and requires that innovators and designers hand over control and authorship of the prototype to the community—something many of them find hard to do.

让所有人参与设计的需求，不仅限于设计思维中的原型设计或 “制作” 阶段。原型设计源于实验理念——研究者或设计师进行测试后解读结果。然而在社会领域，这种模式难以奏效。因为社会领域是个复杂系统，利益相关方与变量众多，且对“不造成伤害”的现实与感知要求都极为敏感。采用单变量研究（如随机对照试验中常见的测试方法）来观察结果过于简单化。原型设计的理念需要融入社区生态，要求创新者与设计师将原型的控制权和所有权移交给社区，而这对许多人而言颇具挑战。



A project the [Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation](http://www.gatesfoundation.org/) funded with the [Chicago Public Library](http://www.chicagotribune.com/bluesky/originals/ct-chicago-public-library-innovation-bsi-20160614-story.html) to [experiment with library design](https://www.chipublib.org/news/chicago-public-library-awarded-1-million-grant-to-pioneer-innovative-library-model/) illustrates how community authorship can work. For one experiment, instead of spending a lot of money and time to design and build a full pilot-level teen lounge, library staff hung up a poster in an area of the library that read, "For the next four weeks, this room will be a teen lounge from this time to this time." They then staged a teen lounge with different activity prompts and equipment, and let the teens discover for themselves when and how to use the space. The teens changed things and added things. It was a living prototype, a living lab; the teen community shaped it. Chicago’s central library later implemented the lounge and has now expanded it to 11 other libraries in the Chicago system. The work also resulted in an [online toolkit for libraries](http://designthinkingforlibraries.com/) seeking to experiment for themselves.

由[比尔及梅琳达·盖茨基金会](http://www.gatesfoundation.org/)资助、[芝加哥公共图书馆](http://www.chicagotribune.com/bluesky/originals/ct-chicago-public-library-innovation-bsi -20160614-story.html)共同开展的[图书馆设计实验](https://www.chipublib.org/news/chicago-public-library-awarded-1-million-grant-to-pioneer-innovative-library-model/)项目，生动展现了社区创作权的实践路径。在某次实验中，图书馆工作人员没有耗费大量资金和时间设计建造完整的青少年休息室，而是在馆内某区域张贴告示：“未来四周，本区域将在指定时段作为青少年休息室开放”。随后他们布置了不同的活动提示和设备，让青少年自主探索空间的使用方式和时间。青少年们不断调整空间布局并增添新元素。这个活生生的原型空间、动态实验室，最终由青少年社群共同塑造而成。芝加哥中央图书馆随后正式推行该休闲区模式，现已扩展至该市系统内的另外11家分馆。该项目还衍生出[图书馆在线工具包](http://designthinkingforlibraries.com/)，供其他图书馆自行开展实践探索。



**Bringing civic designers inside**

**引入公民设计师**

In the for-profit world, some companies, mostly product-based companies, expect all their employees to innovate. But that’s unrealistic for service-based organizations where time and resources are scarce. Large nonprofits might decide to hire outside help and bring the innovations inside once developed, but that approach is more difficult for small and mid-sized organizations.

在商业领域，部分企业（主要是产品型企业）要求全体员工参与创新。但对时间和资源有限的服务型组织而言，这种要求并不现实。大型非营利机构或许会选择聘请外部专家，待创新方案成型后再引入内部，但这种模式对中小型组织而言难度更大。



For most nonprofits, the solution may be in the middle. For example, [Bloomberg Philanthropies](https://www.bloomberg.org/), which focuses on government innovation, funds innovation teams of three to five people who work on horizontal issues at mayor’s offices in more than 20 cities. The teams are comprised of city employees, designers, and outside researchers, and effectively work as internal incubators and accelerators for ideas on transforming city functions. Importantly, these teams never fully own the projects they tackle; they identify who owns a problem internally, and help bring those people along during the process of discovery and design. When the project goes into the pilot or trial phase, the teams make a clean handoff to the owners. Rather than train everybody in design thinking, these teams serve as catalysts and integrators, similar to Hollywood-style movie development teams.

对多数非营利机构而言，折中方案或许是最佳选择。例如专注政府创新的[彭博慈善基金会](https://www.bloomberg.org/)，在20余个城市的市长办公室资助了由3至5人组成的创新团队，负责处理跨部门问题（horizontal issues）。这些团队由市政员工、设计师和外部研究员组成，实质上成为推动城市职能转型的内部孵化器与加速器。关键在于，这些团队并未完全主导其所涉项目；他们会识别内部问题的所有者，并在探索与设计过程中协助其参与。当项目进入试点或试运行阶段，团队便会向所有者完成无缝交接。这些团队并不会培训所有人以掌握设计思维，而是扮演催化剂与整合者的角色（类似好莱坞式的电影开发团队）。



Ultimately, design can help the social sector manage complexity and aid in problem solving, but the sector needs to be creative in how it uses the practice and its methods. Keeping a focus on impact through an activism-centered innovation approach, giving up some control to involve more people, and combining small teams of design thinkers with subject matter experts are just some of the ways organizations can use design to scale.

归根结底，设计思维能助力社会领域应对复杂性并解决问题，但社会领域也需要创新运用实践方法。通过以下方式，组织可实现设计思维的规模化应用：以行动主义为核心的创新路径聚焦社会影响；适度放权以扩大参与度；组建由设计思维引导者与领域专家组成的小型团队。
