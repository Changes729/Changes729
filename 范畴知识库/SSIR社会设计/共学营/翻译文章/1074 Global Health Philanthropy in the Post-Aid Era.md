# Global Health Philanthropy in the Post-Aid Era

# 后援助时代的全球健康慈善事业

Philanthropic capital can't fill the vacuum left by the collapse of international aid, but funders nevertheless need to move from caution to courage and accelerate their pace of giving.

慈善资本无法填补国际援助崩溃留下的空白，但资助者仍需从谨慎之中重拾勇气，加快捐赠的步伐。



![woman standing with a mother and child](https://ssir.org/images/jcogs_img/cache/global-health-philanthropy-592x333_-_abcdef_-_ec76adfb59d23efa05b4d7ecdd5601e6950c9f26.webp)

*Lucia Sanchez Martinez, project coordinator at La Asociación de Campesinos para El Desarrollo Humano (Peasants for Human Development, or CDH), tracks the growth of a young community member. (Photo by Joel Sawady)*

*露西亚·桑切斯·马丁内斯，农民促进人类发展协会（CDH）项目协调员，正伴随一名年轻社区成员的成长。（乔尔·萨瓦迪摄）*



Since January 2025, government cuts to [international development assistance](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ngos-funding-cuts-cost-evidence) have closed clinics, cut jobs, and left communities without care. The toll is mounting: A count in June estimated [124,000 adult and ](https://www.bu.edu/sph/news/articles/2025/tracking-anticipated-deaths-from-usaid-funding-cuts/)259,[000](https://www.impactcounter.com/dashboard?view=table&sort=interval_minutes&order=asc) child deaths as a result.

自2025年1月以来，政府削减了[国际发展援助](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ngos-funding-cuts-cost-evidence)导致诊所关闭、岗位裁撤，使社区陷入医疗保障缺失的状态。灾难已然到来：据六月份的统计显示，[124,000名成人](https://www.bu.edu/sph/news/articles/2025/tracking-anticipated-deaths-from-usaid-funding-cuts/)及[259, 000](https://www.impactcounter.com/dashboard?view=table&sort=interval_minutes&order=asc)名儿童由此丧生。



Even in a best-case scenario, it will take years for most lower- and middle-income countries (LMICs) that received outside support [to step up their own health funding](https://www.cgdev.org/blog/bridging-gap-financing-social-services-after-us-aid-cuts). Philanthropic capital [can’t fill this vacuum](https://borgenproject.org/private-sector-replace-usaid/#:~:text=The USAID freeze has affected,on the ground fighting poverty.). However, US foundations alone hold more than [$1.6 trillion](https://thenonprofittimes.com/npt_articles/foundation-assets-jumped-estimated-170-billion/), US donor-advised funds hold more than $250 billion, and other major global foundations have multi-billion-dollar war chests. And because so many international aid programs have closed or been cut back, a veritable army of skilled global health workers is at hand. For a sense of size, cuts at the United States Agency for International Development (USAID) have led to more than[ 20,000 layoffs in the United States and nearly 240,000 globally](https://www.usaidstopwork.com/). Meanwhile, the World Health Organization deployed [a 21 percent budget cut](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/who-plans-relocations-dropping-some-work-budget-squeeze-2025-08-21/) in May 2025, reducing its management team by half and laying off nearly 400 staff as of August 2025.

即使在最理想的情况下，大多数接受外部援助的中低收入国家（LMICs）也需要数年时间才能[增加本国的卫生资金投入](https://www.cgdev.org/blog/bridging-gap-financing-social-services-after-us-aid-cuts)。慈善资本[无法填补这一缺口](https://borgenproject.org/private-sector-replace-usaid/#:~:text=The USAID freeze has affected,on the ground fighting poverty.)。然而，全美国的基金会就持有超过[1.6万亿美元](https://thenonprofittimes.com/npt_articles/foundation-assets-jumped-estimated-170-billion/)，美国捐赠者建议基金（Donor-advised funds, DAFs）持有逾2500亿美元，其他全球的主要基金会也拥有数十亿美元的储备金。由于众多国际援助项目的关闭或缩减，一支技术娴熟的全球卫生工作者队伍随时待命。为说明严重性：美国国际开发署（USAID）的预算削减导致[美国境内裁员逾2万人，全球近24万人失业](https://www.usaidstopwork.com/)。与此同时，世界卫生组织实施了 [21%的预算削减](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/who-plans-relocations-dropping-some-work-budget-squeeze-2025-08-21/)，截至2025年8月已裁减半数管理团队及近400名员工。

> 捐助者建议基金 (Donor-advised funds, DAFs) 是一种由慈善组织管理的投资账户，允许捐助者获得立即的税收减免，然后建议在以后某个时间分配资金给慈善机构。 



Recent well-intentioned efforts to close the funding gap have yet to attract the capital they need to fully operate. For example, the Center for Global Development’s [Project Resource Optimization](https://proimpact.tools/) initiative connects private donors to a vetted list of high-impact, former USAID partners. It generated \$110 million in funding for various programs [as of September 2025](https://proimpact.tools/funding-opps), but for comparison, [USAID’s congressional appropriation was more than \$35 billion](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF10261) in 2024 alone. Another effort, Unlock Aid’s [Foreign Aid Bridge Fund](https://www.foreignaidbridgefund.org/), raised $2 million from small donors, but couldn’t garner support from large philanthropic partners and shut down in April 2025.

近期旨在填补资金缺口的善行努力，尚未吸引到充分运作所需的资本。例如，全球发展中心的[项目资源优化计划](https://proimpact.tools/)将私人捐助者与经审核的高影响力机构对接，这些机构曾是美国国际开发署的合作伙伴。该计划[截至2025年9月](https://proimpact.tools/funding-opps)为各类项目筹集了1.1亿美元资金，但相比之下，仅2024年[美国国际开发署获得的国会拨款就超过350亿美元](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF10261)。另一项努力——“解锁援助（Unlock Aid）”组织的[对外援助过渡基金](https://www.foreignaidbridgefund.org/)——虽从小型捐赠者处筹集了200万美元，却未能获得大型慈善伙伴的支持，最终于2025年4月关闭。



Two persistent roadblocks stand in the way of efforts like these: 1) philanthropy’s inability to respond quickly to challenges, and 2) funders’ fear of deploying capital without enough information. Surmounting these barriers requires that foundations and other funders set aside traditional tactics and work in new ways. Only by leaning into trust-based philanthropy and recognizing that on-the-ground partners have the knowledge and skillsets to solve their own problems can funders respond in time and increase the pace of progress.

阻碍此类行动的两大障碍是：1）慈善机构应对挑战的反应迟缓；2）资助方因信息不足而畏惧资金投放。要突破这些障碍，基金会等资助方必须摒弃传统策略，开辟全新路径。唯有拥抱基于信任的慈善模式，承认在地合作伙伴具备解决自身问题的知识与技能，资助方才能及时响应并加速进程。



## Impact on the Ground

## 实地影响

When local leaders have resources, they build lasting change. A striking example is *La Asociación de Campesinos para El Desarrollo Humano* (Peasants for Human Development, or CDH), a locally led group in a poor rural community in northeast El Salvador. After their country’s 12-year civil war ended in the early 1990s, international NGOs trained community members as health promoters and lay educators. By the early 2000s, the training ended, and the NGOs left, but these local leaders refused to let care disappear and built an organization from scratch. CDH has thrived for 22 years and now delivers a wide range of services including health care, microfinance lending, water and sanitation improvements, and climate resilient interventions.

当地方领袖拥有资源，他们便能推动持久的变革。萨尔瓦多东北部贫困农村社区的 “农民促进人类发展协会”（La Asociación de Campesinos para El Desarrollo Humano，Peasants for Human Development， CDH）便是鲜明的例证。这个由当地人主导的组织诞生于1990年代初，该国历经十二年内战结束后，当时的国际非政府组织培训了社区成员，使成员成为健康推广员和非专业教育工作者。到21世纪初，培训终止了，非政府组织撤离了，但这些本土的引领者们不希望让这种关怀消失，于是从零开始建立组织。CDH 蓬勃发展至今已22年，如今全方位的提供了涵盖医疗保健、小额信贷、水资源与卫生设施改善和气候韧性干预等服务。



Grassroots organizations like CDH offer safety nets for marginalized communities battling inequality. They are agile and possess the networks and expertise to respond to emerging crises. In our own work as funders and supporters of grassroots organizations, three best practices have proven effective time and again. While these ideas aren’t new, we believe that global philanthropy must embrace them much more earnestly and urgently in this moment:

像 CDH 这样的基层组织为那些对抗不平等（inequality）的边缘化社区提供了安全的网络。它们行动敏捷，拥有应对突发危机的网络资源和专业能力。我们作为基层组织的资助者和支持者，在实践中反复验证了三项最佳实践。尽管这些理念并非新鲜事物，但我们认为，在当下，全球慈善事业必须更加恳切地去接纳它们：



1. Invest in local leaders in LMICs

   投资中低收入国家的地方领袖

2. Make large, unrestricted, multi-year commitments

   提供大额、无限制、多年期的资金承诺

3. Use networks to learn from and identify strong nonprofit partners

   借助网络学习并甄选优质的非营利合作伙伴



## Invest in Local Leaders

## 投资本土领袖

As foreign aid recedes, philanthropy can support grassroots leaders—especially leaders from marginalized groups—as they develop their organizations, grow their skills, and [advocate with local governments](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/system-orchestrators-government-partnerships). Supporting local leadership capacity supports both quick response to changing conditions and long-term change.

随着外国援助的减少，慈善事业可助力基层领袖——尤其是边缘化群体的领袖——他们发展其组织、提升技能，并[开展地方政府倡导工作](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/system-orchestrators-government-partnerships)。支持本土领导力建设，既能支持对变化环境的快速响应，也能推动长期的变革。



An example is José Ramiro Cortez Argueta, known as Ramiro, who trained as a health promoter after the civil war in El Salvador and 10 years later became CDH’s founding executive director. Now, 22 years later, he hires doctors, engineers, accountants, and lawyers, and writes competitive grants for national and international funders, all based on priorities identified within his community. Most recently, he and other CDH leaders have started work on a community-based treatment program in response to rising cases of diabetes and hypertension. The program addresses two major health care issues in LMICs in a manner that’s tailored to the local context. Without the philanthropic capital that supported Ramiro during his time as executive director, CDH would have closed years ago.

何塞·拉米罗·科尔特斯·阿尔圭塔（人称拉米罗）便是一例典范。在萨尔瓦多内战后，他接受了健康促进员培训，十年后成为 CDH 的创始执行负责人。二十二年后的今天，他根据社区的需求聘请医生、工程师、会计师和律师，并为国内外的资助方编写资助申请书。最近，他与其他 CDH 领袖一同，针对日益增多的糖尿病和高血压病例，启动了社区治疗项目。该项目用量身定制的方式，根据当地的实际情况，解决了低中收入国家两大医疗难题。若非慈善资本在拉米罗担任执行负责人期间提供的支持，CDH 可能早就在数年前就关闭了。



Similarly, American Jewish World Service (AJWS) supports thousands of frontline grassroots leaders worldwide through trust-based philanthropy. In March, [Busia Survivors](https://survivors.or.ke/)—a grantee delivering HIV treatment and prevention services in Kenya and Uganda—had to shutter specialized clinics and suspend services due to US foreign assistance cuts. Although government clinics remained open, Busia Survivors’ staff saw that people who previously came to their clinics—including sex workers and other patients vulnerable to stigma and discrimination, and at risk of arrest—stopped seeking out help. In response, the organization quickly negotiated with government hospitals to use two shipping container units as satellite clinics. It then worked with AJWS to draft an agreement with the government to ensure that the clinics remained safe spaces for marginalized patients seeking treatment, contraceptives, counseling, and other support.

类似的，美国犹太世界服务组织（AJWS）通过基于信任的慈善模式支持全球数千名一线基层领袖。今年三月，肯尼亚和乌干达的艾滋病防治服务机构[布西亚幸存者组织（Busia Survivors）](https://survivors.or.ke/)，因美国削减对外援助，而被迫关闭了专科诊所并暂停服务。尽管政府诊所仍在运营，但布西亚幸存者组织的工作人员发现，此前前来就诊的人群（包括性工作者及其他易受污名化、歧视和遭逮捕威胁的患者）已停止（来此）寻求治疗服务。因此，该组织迅速与政府医院协商，将两个集装箱改造成卫星诊所。随后，他们与 AJWS 合作起草了政府协议，确保这些诊所继续成为边缘化患者寻求治疗、避孕措施、医疗门诊及其他支持的安全场所。



## Make Large, Unrestricted, Multi-Year Commitments

## 提供大额、无限制、多年期的资金承诺

Most philanthropists know that [big](https://ssir.org/pdf/Spring2019_BS_Youn.pdf), unrestricted, multi-year gifts stabilize locally led initiatives and give them the flexibility they need to operate resilient, adaptable systems that protect community health. They also signal high confidence to other funders and can create a momentum of giving that helps grassroots organizations thrive. More funders need to actually do it now.

大多数慈善家都深知，[大额](https://ssir.org/pdf/Spring2019_BS_Youn.pdf)、无限制、多年期的捐赠能为地方主导的项目提供稳定支持，赋予它们所需的灵活性支持有韧性和适应力的系统，守护社区健康。此类捐赠还能向其他资助方传递高信任的信号，并构成捐赠的动力，助力基层组织蓬勃发展。当下亟需更多资助方付诸实践。



![girls sitting in a circle on the floor](https://ssir.org/images/jcogs_img/cache/nigee-592x444_-_abcdef_-_ec76adfb59d23efa05b4d7ecdd5601e6950c9f26.webp)

*Girls participate in an advocacy and leadership training at Nyanza Initiative for Girls' Education and Empowerment (NIGEE). (Photo courtesy of NIGEE)*

*女孩们在尼亚萨女童教育与赋权倡议组织（NIGEE）参加倡导与领导力培训。（图片由NIGEE提供）*



Multi-year support has produced several concrete outcomes for CDH. A stable source of income gives leaders and their families a sense of security, drawing local talent to CDH and improving staff retention. It also allows leaders to focus on long-term strategy rather than short-term organizational survival, and to pursue additional funding based on community priorities. Finally, multi-year support allowed CDH to [invest in its own administrative infrastructure](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/the_nonprofit_starvation_cycle), which was then leveraged to secure new international and local government funding for a bridge over a local river, as well as water and sanitation improvements.

多年期支持使 CDH 产生了诸多切实成效：稳定的收入来源使领袖及其家庭获得安全感，吸引当地人才加入 CDH 并提升员工留任率；让管理者得以聚焦长期战略而非短期生存问题，并根据社区的优先事项争取额外的资金； 最后，多年期资助使 CDH 得以[投资自身行政基础设施](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/the_nonprofit_starvation_cycle)，进而撬动新的国际及地方政府资金，用于修建当地河流桥梁以及改善供水和卫生设施。



Another example is AJWS’s long-term, flexible funding of the [Nyanza Initiative for Girls' Education and Empowerment](https://nigee.org/) (NIGEE), which focuses on engaging village elders and local leaders on issues related to sexual health and rights and gender-based violence. After the United States ended its foreign assistance, NIGEE noticed a rise in teenage pregnancies and HIV infections. In response, it paused regular outreach activities and began focusing on training leaders within the community to speak with parents, escort girls to government clinics to acquire contraceptives, and provide counseling. NIGEE’s nearly two decades of experience empowering girls combined with the availability of flexible funding allowed it to respond to the situation immediately; it could pivot with confidence knowing it would still have funding to continue its regular work when the crisis abated.

另一个案例是 AJWS 对[尼亚萨女童教育赋权计划（Nyanza Initiative for Girls' Education and Empowerment，NIGEE）](https://nigee.org/)的长期灵活资助。该计划致力于引导村中长老和地方领袖参与性健康权利及性别暴力议题。美国终止对外援助后，该计划发现青少年怀孕和艾滋病毒感染率上升。作为应对方案，该组织暂停了常规的外展活动，转而重点培训社区领袖开展以下工作：与家长沟通、护送女孩前往政府诊所获取避孕用品、提供心理咨询。NIGEE 近二十年女童赋权的经验积累，加上灵活资金的保障，使其能够迅速应对突发状况——该组织确信危机平息后仍能获得资金支持常规工作，从而得以从容调整工作重心。



## Use Networks

## 借助网络

Some funders are identifying locally led organizations within LMICs and trusting them enough to make unrestricted multi-year commitments. But considering the scale of need, it’s not nearly enough. Tapping into existing networks to vet and connect with new grantee partners is an important piece of the puzzle, and the post-USAID era provides ample opportunities for this. Funders can find organizations within [existing lists](https://proimpact.tools/) of USAID’s [previous](https://www.highergov.com/news/list-of-terminated-usaid-contracts-and-grants-6265029)[ investments](https://www.devex.com/news/the-23-usaid-nonprofit-organizations-that-lost-the-most-109741), many of which are now shutting down for lack of funds. They can also use alliances, such as the [Community Health Impact Coalition](https://joinchic.org/about/) and the [CORE Group](https://coregroup.org/our-work/international-health-network/), and networks established by intermediary funders like AJWS. These groups already have longstanding relationships with locally led partners and a track record of sustained impact.

部分资助方正发掘低收入和中等收入国家中由当地主导的组织，并给予充分信任，向其提供不受限制的多年期资助承诺。但考虑到需求规模，这远远不够。借助现有网络资源来审核并对接新的受助伙伴，是解决难题的关键环节，而 USAID 资助期结束后，这方面正涌现出大量机遇。资助方可通过[现有清单](https://proimpact.tools/)查找美国国际开发署[过往](https://www.highergov.com/news/list-of-terminated-usaid-contracts-and-grants-6265029)的[投资项目](https://www.devex.com/news/ the-23-usaid-nonprofit-organizations-that-lost-the-most-109741)中寻找组织，其中许多机构因资金短缺而面临关闭。他们还可借助联盟组织（如[社区健康影响联盟](https://joinchic.org/about/)和[CORE集团](https://coregroup.org/our-work/international-health-network/)），以及 AJWS 等中介资助方建立的网络。这些团体与本地主导的合作伙伴保持着长期关系，并拥有持续产生影响的实绩。



Funders can also double-down on their support for existing grantees. In March, for example, The Launders Trust renewed grants for [Edesia Nutrition](https://www.edesianutrition.org/?gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=66660872&gbraid=0AAAAADyjeoBvb1UNcLOt9V3pxYBBO3prl&gclid=Cj0KCQjwtMHEBhC-ARIsABua5iSSOnXMofp-0fMU0-I1e3QlHMMsA7Q6YYixatIVTFg5ZTlhdO0o1vcaApOREALw_wcB) and the [Solar Electric Light Fund](https://www.edesianutrition.org/?gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=66660872&gbraid=0AAAAADyjeoBvb1UNcLOt9V3pxYBBO3prl&gclid=Cj0KCQjwtMHEBhC-ARIsABua5iSSOnXMofp-0fMU0-I1e3QlHMMsA7Q6YYixatIVTFg5ZTlhdO0o1vcaApOREALw_wcB), two grantees hit hard by international development cuts. It also plans to award multiyear general operating support to new grantees and find ways to [inspire governments to invest](https://ssir.org/articles/entry/ngos-government-partnership-models-scaling). Meanwhile, several initiatives have emerged as a result of conversations with nonprofit partners, including virtual capacity building workshops on finance and fundraising, convenings to share strategies and resources, and listening sessions where the trust’s staff and trustees learn from grantees.

## Stepping Into the Gap

## 填补缺口

The health infrastructure in many LMICs has been reliant on foreign assistance. Dramatic cuts to funding mean that diseases like HIV and TB will return to places where they were once controlled, emerging diseases will be harder to combat, and the work of addressing chronic illness will lose important champions. And because diseases do not respect borders, vulnerable LMICs leave everyone more vulnerable.

许多中低收入国家的卫生基础设施长期依赖外国援助。资金的大幅削减意味着艾滋病和结核病等疾病将卷土重来，新发传染病的防控将面临更大挑战，慢性病防治工作也将失去重要的支持力量。疾病不分国界，脆弱的中低收入国家将使所有人面临更大风险。



Philanthropy can and must help mitigate these threats now by partnering with local leaders, using multi-year investments, and leveraging networks. The money is there. Health experts are ready. If funders wait for perfect conditions, they will lose the opportunity to save lives. If they act with courage, they can seize the moment.

慈善力量此刻必须通过与当地领袖合作、实施多年期投资、整合资源网络来缓解这些威胁。资金已到位，医疗专家已就绪。若资助方还在等待完美条件，将错失拯救生命的良机；若勇于行动，便能把握当下。
