# Beyond‘Toughing It Out“：Mental Health in the Social Change Workplace

# 无需 “咬紧牙关”：社会变革工作场所中的心理健康

Integrating mental health in social change workplaces allows us to cultivate new narratives and norms that will better sustain long-term engagement with social change.

在社会变革的工作环境中融入心理健康理念，能让我们培育新的叙事与规范，从而更好地维持对社会变革的长期投入。



The applause at the conference was enthusiastic, and the ambiance, reflective. Hands shot up in the air with question after question:

“Can you say more about how it felt when you were depressed?”

“What can we do if our CEO is not supportive of employee well-being?”

“My friend has bipolar, how can I support him?”

会议现场掌声热烈，而气氛却很沉静。提问的手接连举起：

“您能否阐述更多关于您抑郁时的感受？”
“在我们CEO不重视员工福祉的情况下，我们该怎么做？”
“我的朋友患有双相情感障碍，我该如何帮助他？”



I (Enoch) gladly and patiently answered them. On the outside, I looked poised, having just shared my lived experience from depression and suicide attempts to founding my social impact consulting company, [Bearapy](http://bearapy.me/), to improve workplace mental health in the Asia-Pacific region. Inside, I could feel myself disintegrating. I knew my energy was depleting, and I had been met with rejection after rejection from potential clients because they did not have the “budget” for mental well-being initiatives. I was full of doubt, and wondered if leaving my career as a corporate banker to become a social entrepreneur was a wise choice.

我（伊诺克）欣然且耐心地作答。表面上，我神情自若——刚分享完从抑郁自杀未遂到创立社会影响力咨询公司[小熊疗愈（Bearapy）](http://bearapy.me/)的亲身经历，致力于改善亚太地区职场心理健康。但内心深处，我却感到自己的精神略显崩溃。我清楚地意识到自己的精力正在枯竭，因为被潜在客户接连拒绝合作，理由是他们并没有“预算” 投入心理健康项目。我满心怀疑，不禁质疑当初放弃企业银行家的职业生涯转而投身社会创业是否明智。



Many social change makers choose to dedicate our careers to the sector despite the hardships, driven by a sense of purpose. But the hardships are not any less real. Under the immense pressure to build a sustainable venture and make a meaningful impact, we risk forgetting ourselves. This work takes a toll on our mental health.

许多社会变革者选择将职业生涯奉献给这个领域，尽管艰辛重重，但我们始终怀揣着使命感前行。然而这些艰辛同样真实存在。在建立可持续事业并创造深远影响的巨大压力下，我们面临着迷失自我的风险。这份工作对我们的心理健康造成了沉重打击。



As with Enoch, I (Daisy) have seen social change leaders, including myself, suffer under the weight of immense burdens: the struggle for sufficient resources, the injustices we tackle, the suffering we witness, the desire to be seen and accompanied, the demand to constantly prove ourselves worthy of the work. I have had countless conversations with [Brio’s](https://startbrio.org/) partner teams about the ways we dwell on the past or worry about the future—all while struggling to be fully engaged in the work at present. Added to this is the context of all the systemic barriers and cultural norms that go to the very core of the social change we are trying to effect.

正如伊诺克所经历的，我（黛西）目睹了包括自己在内的许多社会变革领袖们所承受着压力：资源匮乏的挣扎、抗争不公的煎熬、目睹苦难的痛楚、渴望被看见与陪伴，以及必须不断证明自己配得上这份事业的压力。我与[Brio](https://startbrio.org/)的合作伙伴团队进行过无数次对话，探讨我们如何沉溺于过去或忧虑未来——同时又在当下全情投入工作。更棘手的是，我们面临的系统性障碍与文化规范，恰恰构成我们试图改变的社会变革的核心症结。



We need new structures and narratives in social change. We also need new emotional responses to the undeniably difficult endeavors we face. In other words, in order to catalyze change, we’ll also have to change ourselves.

社会变革需要新的结构与叙事，更需要面对艰巨挑战时的新型情感应对机制。换言之，要催化变革，我们自身也必须先要改变。



## Acknowledging the Emotional Hardships in Social Change

## 承认社会变革中的心理困境

It is no secret that the work of social change is hard on social change makers. Numerous studies have demonstrated the [higher prevalence of stress](https://humanfactors101.files.wordpress.com/2021/01/work-related-stress-anxiety-or-depression-humanfactors101.com_.pdf) among those who work in public service industries and caring professions, including educators, youth workers, and health workers. These teams directly contend with the immediate aftermath and lasting impacts of poverty, violence, and other realities in systemically under-resourced contexts. Social change makers with marginalized identities experience further strain as we navigate dominating culture and oppressive systems of power.

社会变革工作对变革推动者造成巨大压力，这已不是什么秘密。大量研究表明，公共服务行业及关怀类职业从业者——包括教育工作者、青年工作者和医疗工作者——[会有更高的压力患病率](https://humanfactors101.files.wordpress.com/2021/01/work-related-stress-anxiety-or-depression-humanfactors101.com_.pdf)。这些团队在系统性资源匮乏的环境中，直接面对贫困、暴力等现实问题带来的即时后果与长期影响。具有边缘化身份的社会变革者更承受着双重压力——既要应对主流文化，又要抗衡压迫性权力体系。



And yet, we see too many social change workers minimize or even deny the effects of these hardships by comparing their circumstances to those of the people they serve, or telling themselves to “tough it out.” We have internalized grind culture and assume we can push through our difficult experiences with sheer willpower. Despite these efforts, emotional pain and physical pain [activate us in similar ways](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/body-sense/201204/emotional-and-physical-pain-activate-similar-brain-regions), and we cannot ignore the pain that exists in our sector, on our teams, and within ourselves. This is self-exploitation—and our unaddressed pain inevitably wears us down.

然而，我们目睹了太多社会变革工作者通过将自身处境与服务对象相比较，或告诫自己要“咬牙坚持”，来淡化甚至否认这些困境的影响。我们已将“拼命工作的文化”内化，误以为仅凭意志力就能突破困境。尽管如此，情感痛苦与身体痛苦[以相似方式影响着我们](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/body-sense/201204/emotional-and-physical-pain-activate-similar-brain-regions)，我们无法忽视行业内、团队中以及自身存在的痛苦。这就是一种自我剥削——那些未被正视的伤痛终将耗尽我们的生命力。



Furthermore, stressful workplace environments that lack appropriate supports can lead to negative outward consequences. According to the [International Labour Organization](https://www.ilo.org/wcmsp5/groups/public/---ed_protect/---protrav/---safework/documents/publication/wcms_466547.pdf), increased worker absenteeism, reduced motivation, decreased satisfaction and creativity, and increased staff turnover are a few of the challenges faced by organizations without supportive psychosocial environments. The case is clear for promoting mental health in every workplace—and all the more in the social change sector, where the context of the work itself is reliably intense and difficult.

此外，缺乏适当支持的压力性工作环境可能会导致消极的外在后果。根据[国际劳工组织](https://www.ilo.org/wcmsp5/groups/public/-- -ed_protect/---protrav/---safework/documents/publication/wcms_466547.pdf)指出，缺乏社会心理（psychosocial）支持环境的组织将面临多重挑战：员工缺勤率上升、工作积极性下降、满意度与创造力降低、人员流动率攀升。在每个工作场所促进心理健康都具有明确意义——社会变革领域尤为如此，该领域的工作本身就已足够高度紧张和困难。



Ultimately, while there is [mounting evidence](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336956034_Promoting_Mentally_Healthy_Workplaces) correlating positive mental health and worker effectiveness, our argument for supporting mental health in the social change workplace is rooted in human dignity and self-inclusion. Mental health is a fundamental human right—and is indeed necessary for navigating life’s challenges toward a meaningful contribution.

尽管现有证据日益表明心理健康与工作效能存在[正相关关系](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336956034_Promoting_Mentally_Healthy_Workplaces)，但我们在社会变革领域倡导心理健康支持的根本依据，在于捍卫人的尊严与自我认同。心理健康是基本人权——更是人们应对人生挑战、实现具有意义的人生贡献的必要条件。



## How to Build Skills, Shift Behavior, and Change Culture

## 如何培养技能、转变行为、改变文化

The challenges inherent to social impact work are unlikely to disappear. Yet there are specific mental health practices we can cultivate in our workplace to expand our behavioral possibilities, loosen our rigid narratives, and free up energy to seek meaningful systemic and cultural impact. Here are some ideas for how to get started.

社会影响力工作中固有的挑战难以消除。然而，我们可以在职场中培养特定的心理健康实践，拓展行为可能性，打破僵化的思维模式，释放能量以寻求有意义的系统性与文化性影响。以下是一些实践建议：



### 1. Cultivate shared language around mental and emotional experiences.

### 1. 建立关于心理与情感体验的共同语言

There is a common misconception that people do not want to talk about mental health due to “stigma” and “taboos.” While this is real and more pertinent in some cultures than others, this is not universally true, and if we allow ourselves some creativity, we can find a shared language that allows us to express while being sensitive.

普遍存在一种误解：人们因“耻辱”和“禁忌”的原因而不愿意谈论心理健康。虽然这种现象确实存在，且在某些文化中更为突出，但并非放之四海皆准。只要发挥创造力，我们就能找到既能表达自我又保持敏感的共同语言。



In our work at Bearapy, we facilitate workshops mainly in Asia-Pacific. A common context we share is that many of us were taught to be achievement-focused and task-oriented. Expressing emotions is a less-developed muscle. So, instead of accepting this simply as a “cultural issue,” we supply the words, literally. We lead executives gently by explaining the significance of emotions to our self-awareness and professional development. We give them a list of common emotions of different ranges and intensities, and encourage a method that is simplistic and yet awkward at first attempt, which is to articulate: “I feel disappointed, lonely, frustrated, betrayed, joyful, triumphant, hopeful, etc.” Then, we ask them to connect situations with the emotional experience, and how that relates to their feelings of stress and anxiety as an opening to discuss relevant mental health topics.

在小熊疗愈（Bearapy）的工作中，我们主要在亚太地区开展工作坊。我们共同面临的背景是：许多人从小被教导要以成就为导向、以任务为中心，情感表达能力相对薄弱。因此，我们不将此简单归为“文化问题”，而是直接提供表达工具。通过阐释情感对自我认知和职业发展的意义，我们引导企业高管逐步建立情感表达能力。我们提供涵盖不同层次与强度的常见情绪清单，并鼓励采用一种看似简单，但初次尝试时会略显笨拙的方法：清晰地表达 “我感到失望、孤独、沮丧、背叛、喜悦、胜利、希望等”。随后引导他们将具体情境与情绪体验关联，进而探讨这些感受如何引发压力与焦虑，以此开启心理健康议题的讨论。



This is where the behaviors change—they start talking and do not want to stop.

正是这一刻，行为开始转变——他们开始畅所欲言，且欲罢不能。



### 2. Help managers know what to listen for and ask better questions.

### 2. 帮助管理者掌握倾听要点并提出更有效的问题。

Many managers share the same grievance: “But they don’t tell me they need help!” Ideally, a team member would be extremely articulate and also have the courage to knock on your door and tell you they need time off to tend to their mental health. Realistically, sometimes the team member themself lacks the full understanding of their experience—let alone the words for it. We need to listen between the lines.

许多管理者都有同样的困扰：“可是员工从来不会主动寻求帮助！” 在理想情况下，团队成员应能清晰表达需求，并勇于敲开你的办公室门，坦诚告知需要休假来调理心理健康。但现实中，有时成员对自身经历的认知尚不完整——更遑论用言语准确描述。我们需要学会读懂言外之意。



Team members might share their questions, doubts, and concerns through casual conversations and questions. For example, they may disclose a challenging situation with a family member as a way of speaking indirectly about a deeply difficult emotional experience. When we misperceive their disclosure as a problem to be solved, we miss the opportunity to listen deeply to the emotions and state of mental health expressed. Sometimes, these are the indirect, indistinct calls for help. The psychosocial education we need to nurture is how to hear the clues, and how to integrate reinforcing knowledge with flexible space for colleagues to express themselves.

团队成员可能通过闲谈和提问传递疑问、疑虑与困扰。例如，他们可能借家人困境的间接表达，暗指深陷的情感困境。若我们将这些倾诉误解为待解决的问题，便错失了深入聆听其情感状态与心理健康状况的机会。有时，这些正是隐晦的求助信号。我们亟需培养的心理社会教育，在于如何捕捉这些线索，如何将专业知识与灵活的表达空间相结合，为同事创造倾诉空间。



The art is to listen for clues, and to relate to their subconscious worries as an opening for a conversation about what’s next.

真正的艺术在于：倾听中的洞察，要以共情其潜在的忧虑为切入点，以开辟进一步的对谈。



### 3. Connect mental health to personal, cultural, and organizational values.

### 3. 将心理健康与个人、文化及组织价值观相联结。

For every team member who is eager to discuss the importance of mental health, there will be other team members who are less inclined to do so. While mental and emotional pain is a universal human experience, it is also unique to each individual. We would do ourselves no favors insisting that colleagues or community members engage with mental health in the same exact ways. So, we must find a way to adapt.

每当有团队成员热切探讨心理健康的重要性时，总会有其他成员对此缺乏兴致。尽管心理和情感痛苦是人类都会有的体验，但它对每个人而言又是独一无二的。若强求同事或社区成员以完全相同的方式面对心理健康问题，只会适得其反。因此我们必须寻求灵活应对之道。



For example, when I (Enoch) was discussing mental health with a group of young professionals here in China, many mentioned the loss of “face” if they fell ill or were burned out, and hence their reluctance towards the topic. We picked up on that, and flipped it. We did not tell them they did not have to worry about “face” or that it was old-fashioned, because we understand it is extremely ingrained in the societal context. Rather, we used the concept. We encouraged them that looking after one’s health is a way to give us more “face,” because once we are healthy, buoyant, and fit, we are more productive, which then means there is a higher chance of reaching the goals we have set for ourselves. Thus, the conversation continued with them exploring what that meant for themselves.

例如当我（伊诺克）在中国与一群年轻的专业人士探讨心理健康时，许多人提及生病或精疲力竭会然让他们 “丢脸”，因而对这个话题避而不谈。我们捕捉到这个痛点并巧妙转化。并未直接阐明说到 “不必顾虑面子” 或 “这是陈旧观念” ——因为我们深知这种观念已深深植根于社会语境。相反，我们巧妙运用了这个概念。我们鼓励他们认识到：守护健康恰恰能为我们赢得更多“面子”——当身心健康、精神饱满时，工作效率会自然提升，实现个人目标的概率随之增加。由此，对话深入到他们各自对这一理念的探索。



Try intrinsic motivation by considering how a commitment to supporting mental health aligns with one’s chosen quality of being.

不妨尝试激发内在动力：思考对心理健康的承诺如何与个人追求的生活品质相契合。



### 4. Develop awareness of the painful narratives that affect your team.

### 4. 培养对影响团队的消极叙事的认知

As part of a broader social change ecosystem, I (Daisy) have encountered numerous social entrepreneurs and leaders who are affected, and deflated, by narrow notions of success. Powerful, prominent voices in our sector often dictate what is considered “real” impact, even when marginalized voices may say otherwise. Here are a few common examples of narratives that discourage, and even hinder, teams doing important work:

作为广泛的社会变革生态系统的一员，我（黛西）接触过众多社会企业家和领袖，他们深受狭隘成功观念的影响而备受打击。即便边缘群体持不同观点，我们领域内那些强势而显赫的声音往往主导着所谓“真实”影响力的定义。以下是几种常见的叙事模式，它们会打击甚至阻碍团队开展重要工作：

- Obsession with scale: *We haven’t reached huge numbers of people so our work must not be important.*

  规模迷思：*我们尚未触达海量人群，因此工作必然不重要。*

- Gatekeeping: *We lack the right recognition or qualifications and therefore are not worthy to be at the table.*

  门槛思维：*我们缺乏应有的认识和资质，因此不配参与决策。*

- Comparison: *We cannot complain about how hard this is because some people in our community have it much worse.*

  比较心态：*我们不能抱怨工作艰辛，因为社区里还有人处境更艰难。*

- Heropreneurship: *We must bear the weight of this work on our own.*

  英雄主义创业观：*必须独自扛起这项工作的重担*

- Failure: *Not an option.*

  失败禁忌：*失败是不可接受的（根本不能考虑失败）*

Even if we do not say them out loud, we must become aware, as compassionately as possible, that we operate under the influence of such narratives. Then, we can explore which of these ideas we perpetuate, and what might be possible if we start to tell new stories about the work we do and what it means.

即便我们未曾言明，也需要以最大程度的同理心意识到：这些叙事正潜移默化地影响着我们的行动。继而我们可以反思自身延续了哪些观念，并探索当我们开始为工作及其意义编织新故事时，可能开启怎样的可能。



Naming harmful narratives and acknowledging how they affect us can be the first step to changing our relationship with the work we do. We experience freedom when we recognize that we can simply observe and be curious about these narratives, rather than cling to them as rigid rules.

识别有害叙事并承认其影响，是改变我们与工作关系的第一步。当我们意识到可以单纯观察并好奇这些叙事，而非将其视为刻板的制度时，自由便油然而生。



### 5. Offer appropriate support and cultivate positive mental health together.

### 5. 提供适当支持，共同培育积极心理健康

A common starting point for companies and organizations seeking to support mental health is to offer access to therapy, coaching, and other related services. They may also consider offerings such as mental health days, personal development funding, wellness programs, and sabbaticals. It is important to note the critical role of providing access to mental health professionals; managers and colleagues should never be expected to fill this role. If someone at the workplace needs professional support, or is in crisis, we must utilize the resources available to us. Many workplaces use models such as [V-A-R](https://www.activeminds.org/about-mental-health/var/) (Validate, Appreciate, Refer) to handle crisis situations and ascertain that individuals are referred to the right kind of support.

企业与组织支持心理健康的常见起点是提供心理治疗、辅导及其他相关服务。还可考虑推出心理健康日、个人发展资助、健康计划及休假制度等举措。需特别强调的是，确保员工能接触专业心理健康服务至关重要——内部的管理者和同事绝不应该被要求承担此类职责。当职场成员需要专业支持或处于（心理）危机状态时，必须调动现有资源。许多企业采用[V-A-R](https://www.activeminds.org/about-mental-health/var/)模式（即验证、认可、转介）处理危机情境，确保当事人获得恰当支持。



In addition to helping individuals access care as needed, consider making it possible for them to build mental health skills for personal and professional well-being. These opportunities might include facilitated well-being spaces or thematic conversations where people can gather to explore important topics together. They can start with simple questions such as, *What is important to you right now? What seems to be getting in the way? What have you noticed about your responses to what’s happening? How would you like to respond?* After working with teams serving some of the world’s most marginalized communities, I (Daisy) have learned that these kinds of conversations can be deeply meaningful, supportive, and vitalizing. They also offer an intentional space to practice the ideas we have suggested above.

除协助员工获取必要的照顾之外，还应创造条件帮助其培养心理健康技能，以促进个人与职业发展。可设立引导式健康空间或主题交流会，让员工共同探讨重要议题。可从简单问题切入：*当下对你而言什么最重要？哪些因素阻碍了你？你注意到自己对现状有何反应？希望如何应对？*在服务全球边缘化群体的团队中工作后，我（黛西）深切体会到：这类对话能带来深刻的意义感、支持力与活力。它们也为实践上述理念提供了刻意营造的空间。



Working toward social change has not been, and never will be, easy. Sometimes, our work takes us to extreme levels of distress and depths of despair; other times, the daily ins and outs are enough to cause us to question our commitment to the mission. These experiences are a heavy burden for anyone to bear, especially alone.

推动社会变革从来不易，未来亦然。有时工作会将我们推向极度痛苦与绝望的深渊；有时日常琐事便足以动摇我们对使命的信念。这些经历对任何人都是沉重负担，独自承受尤甚。



If mental health ultimately allows us to navigate life’s difficulties toward a sense of growth and contribution, then it is critical to work that exists at the intersection of our collective pain and our collective freedom. By embedding simple practices to support mental health, and cultivating a new social change culture that includes our own well-being, we have a better chance at sustaining our engagement in difficult but meaningful work.

若心理健康终能引领我们穿越人生困境，获得成长与贡献的实感，那么它便是连接集体痛苦与集体自由的关键纽带。通过融入支持心理健康的简单实践，培育兼顾个体福祉的新型社会变革文化，我们才能更有力地持续投身于艰难而意义非凡的事业。
