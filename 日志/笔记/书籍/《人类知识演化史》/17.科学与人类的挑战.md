# ***Chapter 17***

# Science and the Challenges of Humanity

# 科学与人类的挑战

> Natural science will in time incorporate into itself the science of man, just as the science of man will incorporate into itself natural science: there will be *one* science.
>
> 自然科学往后将包括关于人的科学，正像关于人的科学包括自然科学一样：这将是一门科学。
>
> —KARL MARX, *Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844*
>
> ——卡尔·马克思《1844年经济学哲学手稿》

> The voice of the intellect is a soft one, but it does not rest till it has gained a hearing. Finally, after a countless succession of rebuffs, it succeeds. This is one of the few points on which one may be optimistic about the future of mankind, but it is in itself a point of no small importance.
>
> 理智的声音是轻柔的，但它在获得聆听之前不会停止。最后，经过无数次的回击，它终于成功了。这是少数几个可以让人对人类未来感到乐观的观点之一，但它本身就具有非同小可的重要性。
>
> —SIGMUND FREUD, “The Future of an Illusion” 
>
> ——西格蒙德·弗洛伊德《一个幻觉的未来》

We need, I believe, to realign science with the challenges of humanity. This request is, however, neither new nor entirely safe because of the risks it entails. It might be seen as a revival of the early modern alliance between science and the quest to re-center the position of humanity in the world. Then and now, challenging problems required new forms of knowledge integration. **In early modern Europe, the challenging objects of science were generated by the great engineering ventures**, which made it necessary to assemble all available knowledge resources, from Greek mathematics to the practical experience of contemporary engineers.

我认为，我们需要让科学重新应对人类面临的挑战。然而，这一愿景既不新颖，也不完全安全，因为它会带来风险。它可以被看作在现代早期，科学与追求人类中心主义间的联盟的复兴。无论过去还是现在，具有挑战性的问题都需要新形式的知识整合。**在近代早期的欧洲，科学的挑战对象是由宏伟的工程所产生的**，这就需要汇集所有可用的知识资源，从希腊数学到当代工程师的实践经验。



**In our period, the grand challenges are encountered in their aftermath.** They no longer concern the local fate of city-states but all of global society under the conditions of the Anthropocene. Accordingly, our perspective is no longer one of infinite horizons and new worlds. Rather, **it is focused on the limits, the intrinsic complexities, and the historical dynamics of systems**, be they ecological, societal, or cognitive. The main concern of the present is no longer one of <u>universalizing the local</u> (as it was in the early modern period) but of <u>localizing and contextualizing the supposedly universal</u>. We can no longer categorically segregate culture from nature but must face the fact that these spheres are inescapably mingled.

**在我们这个时代，巨大的挑战是在其后果中遇到的。**它们不再关乎如城邦一样的地方命运，而是关乎人类世条件下的全球社会。因此，我们的视角不再是无限的视野和崭新的世界，与之相反，**视角因聚焦于系统的局限性、内在复杂性及其历史动力**，无论是生态、社会还是认知系统。当下的主要关注点不再是<u>地方的普遍化（universalizing）</u>（就像现代早期那样），而是所谓<u>普遍性的本地化（localizing）和情景化（contextualizing）</u>。我们不能再将文化与自然截然分开，而必须正视这些领域不可避免地相互交融这一事实。



In the early modern period, artisans, artists, engineers, scientists, and philosophers struggled to overcome traditional boundaries. **Today we require no less courage in removing boundaries of a new kind—on both the individual and institutional levels—when we explore spheres of knowledge hitherto divided in order to exploit the potential of science as a medium of reflection in a globalized world.** A reflective approach to scientific practice will be crucial for regaining control of the seemingly insuperable dynamic that is bringing about the Anthropocene—a dynamic that may threaten the very conditions of our existence.

在近代早期，工匠、艺术家、工程师、科学家和哲学家都在努力克服传统的界限。**今天，我们同样需要勇气来破除新的困境——同样适用于个人与机构组织，以便在全球化的世界中，通过探索细分的知识领域，挖掘科学作为反思媒介的潜力。**对科学实践采用反思的方法对于重新掌控人类世面临的无法克服的变化（dynamic）至关重要——这种变化（dynamic）可能会威胁到我们的生存条件。

The early modern engineer-scientists’ tradition of engagement with practical challenges was, in a way, continued in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries by hybrid experts producing useful knowledge with the intention of improving the human condition. Figures such as Alexander von Humboldt were not only engaged in solving societal problems by contributing to scientific knowledge and technical innovation; they also appreciated the political and, in his case, global dimension of that engagement.

在某种程度上，18 世纪和 19 世纪，混合专家延续了早期现代工程师-科学家应对实际挑战的传统，他们生产有用的知识，旨在改善人类的生活条件。亚历山大-冯-洪堡（Alexander von Humboldt）等人不仅通过贡献科学知识和技术创新来解决社会问题，他们还重视这种参与的政治性和全球性。



There are, however, other examples that may raise skepticism as to the wisdom of bending science toward a broader engagement with civil and political issues. The engagement of scientists in problematic and ultimately murderous political movements, such as the eugenics movement in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, highlights the dangers of crossing the boundary between science and politics—to say nothing of the crimes against humanity committed by scientists in the name of Nazi or other ideologies. Can we, in light of this, truly claim that science may still serve as a beacon of life and the survival of the human species?

然而，还有其他一些例子可能会让人怀疑，让科学更广泛地参与公民和政治问题是否明智。19 世纪末 20 世纪初的优生学运动等有问题并最终导致谋杀的政治运动中，科学家的参与凸显了跨越科学与政治之间界限的危险，更不用说科学家以纳粹或其他意识形态的名义犯下的危害人类罪了。有鉴于此，我们还能真正声称科学仍然是生命和人类生存的灯塔吗？

In addressing the ambiguities of such requests (which fall within the tradition of the Enlightenment) we may learn from the history of religions—not a simple task, and one that may be easily misunderstood. But I do not wish to erase the dividing lines between science and religion. **Knowledge, critique, and self-criticism—often believed to be hallmarks of science**—are not alien to religious traditions. Religions, however, are not primarily concerned with the institutionalized production and critical evaluation of knowledge; in the sense used in this book, science is. Traditionally, religions have offered life orientation to large communities and have even claimed to do so for all of humanity. Religions transmit basic human experiences and offer individuals participation not only in a community providing collective identity but, in a sense, also in the fate of humanity as a whole.[3](#1970592) **Is it possible that science could offer such a participatory perspective on the fate of humanity as well?**

在处理这些要求（属于启蒙运动的传统范畴）的模糊性时，我们可以从宗教史中学习——这不是一项简单的任务，而且很容易被误解。但我并不想抹去科学与宗教之间的分界线。**知识、批判和自我批判——通常被认为是科学的标志**——对宗教传统而言并不陌生。然而，宗教主要关注的并不是知识的制度化生产和批判性评价；以本书的视角来看，科学才是。传统上，宗教为大型社区提供生活导向，甚至声称为全人类提供生活导向。**科学也有可能从人类命运的角度提供这样一种类似的视角吗？**



In the face of the complexity of human experience in a modernized and globalized world, traditional religions are increasingly incapable of offering orientation in so broad a sense, in particular one based on comprehensive knowledge about this world. This incapability may lead to a withdrawal from the challenges of this world or to violent attempts at organizing life itself according to the relatively narrow orientation that traditional religions frequently offer, based on the more limited stock of experiences that constitute their core. From a secular point of view, this shortcoming suggests reducing religions to a historically obsolete source of ethical reflection, thus ignoring **their power to create and maintain strong communities held together by common practices, beliefs, and knowledge.**

面对现代化和全球化世界中人类经验的复杂性，传统宗教越来越无法提供如此广义上的定位，尤其是基于对这个世界的全面了解。这种无能可能会导致人们逃避这个世界的挑战，或者以暴力的方式试图按照传统宗教经常提供的相对狭隘的方向来组织生活，而这种视角归根到底是因为经验储备的有限性。从世俗的角度来看，这种缺陷意味着将宗教的伦理反思贬低为过时的（思想）渊源，从而忽视了它们**创造和维护由共同习俗、信仰和知识维系在一起的强大社群的力量。**

From an Enlightenment perspective, we have become accustomed to measuring religion by the standard of science. Religion is thereby seen, at best, as a cultural catchall, responsible for the final questions of life and death and for preserving traditional identities, values, customs, and ways of coping with the challenges of life. What is easily overlooked from this perspective, however, is the millennia of guidance that religions have offered individuals and societies across the globe—might this not help us formulate questions about the potential of science to orient humanity? For example: Does science employ accumulated human experience to address questions concerning humanity’s survival in the Anthropocene? Does it contribute to making human societies self-aware? Does it enable individuals to contribute to shaping the fate of humanity (which is determined in no small measure by science itself)?

从启蒙运动的角度来看，我们已经习惯于用科学的标准来衡量宗教。因此，宗教充其量被视为一种文化包罗万象，负责解决生与死的终极问题，维护传统身份、价值观、习俗和应对生活挑战的方式。然而，从这个角度来看，宗教为全球个人和社会提供长达千年指引却很容易被忽视——这难道不能启发我们思考，关于科学是否具有为人类指明方向的潜力？比如说科学是否利用人类积累的经验来解决人类在 “人类世 ”的生存问题？科学是否有助于提高人类社会的自我意识？它是否使个人能够为塑造人类命运（这在很大程度上是由科学本身决定的）做出贡献？



In Christian theology, eschatology is concerned with the ultimate destiny of humanity. Today, as the destiny of humanity cannot be separated from the knowledge of science and technology, we may seek out the eschatological dimensions of science itself and cultivate its role as a guide in a fragile world whose future depends on it.

在基督教神学中，末世论关注的是人类的终极命运。今天，由于人类的命运离不开科学技术知识，我们可以探寻科学本身的末世论内涵，并在这个未来取决于科学的脆弱世界中培养科学的指导作用。



There can be no constructive answers to these questions as long as we view science as a mere mountain of facts whose peaks offer no vistas, and as long as we do not lift the heavy veil of inevitability that sometimes seems to weigh it down with the illusion of blind instrumental rationality. On the other hand, the preceding chapters have shown us that **science is just the tip of an iceberg whose substance is the knowledge of the world and whose shape is subject to constant change.** We have come to identify **reflection (thinking about thinking) as the essential mechanism that makes this structural change of knowledge possible.** The categories that shape our understanding of the world are therefore not stuck in the straitjacket of a predetermined logic—they are, at least in principle, constantly subject to change under our evaluation. Meanwhile, scientific knowledge has become the bottom of the iceberg as well, first because it has become relevant to virtually all domains of society and, more important, because its capacity to deliver the knowledge society needs has become a matter of survival.

只要我们把科学看作是一座堆积事实的山——而山顶却看不到任何远景；只要我们不揭开那层沉重的、不可避免的面纱，有时它似乎被盲目的工具理性的错觉所隐埋，那么我们就不可能对这些问题给出建设性的答案。另一方面，前几章已经向我们表明，**科学只是冰山一角，其本质是对世界的认识，而其形状则是不断变化的。**我们已经认识到，**反思（关于思考的思考）是使知识的这种结构性变化成为可能的基本机制。**因此，塑造我们对世界的理解的范畴并不拘泥于预定逻辑的束缚——它们至少在原则上是可以在我们的评价下不断变化的。与此同时，科学知识也已成为冰山的底部，这首先是因为科学知识几乎与社会的所有领域都息息相关，更重要的是，科学知识提供社会所需的知识的能力已成为生存问题。

In some periods in world history, science or scholarship and erudition have served as a “quasi-religion”—as an elitist religion for experts, for instance, or as an all-encompassing salvific enterprise, or as an institutionalized church. Let us briefly consider these different options one by one. Religions for experts are typically the constructions of elites who see themselves as an intellectual aristocracy, aloof from the masses and their trivial beliefs. **This type of quasi-religious image of knowledge is as old as the separation of intellectual and manual labor;** it was already found among the Mesopotamian and Egyptian scribes, and later (as we saw in [chapter 3](#177429)) among Greek philosophers. **The view of science as a mystery to be revealed only to a few chosen people often goes hand in hand with the prospect of abandoning those who do not understand it—an attitude that has wed scientists to criminal regimes in the past.**

在世界历史的某些时期，科学或学术和博学充当了 “准宗教 ”的角色——例如，作为专家的精英宗教，或作为包罗万象的救赎事业，或作为制度化的教会。让我们逐一简述这些不同的选择。专家宗教通常是精英们的产物，他们将自己视为知识贵族，远离大众及其琐碎的信仰。**这种知识的准宗教形象与智力劳动和体力劳动的分离一样古老**；在美索不达米亚和埃及的文士中已经出现，后来（正如我们在 [chapter 3](#177429) 中看到的那样）在希腊哲学家中也出现过。**将科学视为只向少数被选中的人揭示的奥秘的观点，往往与抛弃那些不理解科学的人的前景并行不悖——这种态度在过去曾让科学家与罪恶政权结下了不解之缘。**

In contrast, in the early modern period we see science being pursued within a “workshop of hopes,” as part of an all-encompassing, inner-worldly salvific enterprise directed at all of humanity, in parallel and often in competition with the great religious upheavals of the time. Early modern science was not only characterized by epistemic and economic transformations as I have discussed them; it also stirred numerous hopes for improving life, and not just for the scientists. The philosopher, pedagogue, and theologian Jan Amos Comenius, for instance, engaged himself in universal education and proposed a “General Consultation on the Improvement of Human Affairs,” which is also the title of his principal work. Among science’s eschatological characteristics—in the sense of the ultimate hopes it raised—were its claim to productivity (i.e., that its value should be judged by its fruits); its inductive character (i.e., the individual is conceived as a representative of the whole, by which the latter than stands or falls); its immanence (i.e., its claim to explain the world of its own accord); and its inclusivity (i.e., the claim that its knowledge is able, as Descartes put it, “to secure the general welfare of mankind”).

与此相反，在近代早期，我们看到科学是在 “希望的工作坊 ”中进行的，它是针对全人类的、包罗万象的、内在世界的拯救事业的一部分，与当时的宗教大动荡并行不悖，而且往往相互竞争。早期现代科学的特点不仅仅是我所讨论的认识论和经济学的变革，它还激起了无数改善生活的希望，而不仅仅是科学家的希望。例如，哲学家、教育家和神学家扬-阿莫斯-夸美纽斯（Jan Amos Comenius）致力于普及教育，并提出了 “关于改善人类事务的一般性协商”，这也是他主要著作的标题。科学的末世论特征——从它所提出的终极希望的意义上来说——包括它对生产力的要求（即应根据其成果来判断其价值）；它的归纳性（即个人被视为人类的代表）、 个人被视为整体的代表，而整体的兴衰则取决于个人）；它的内在性（即它声称能够自行解释世界）；以及它的包容性（即正如笛卡尔所说，它的知识能够 “确保人类的普遍福祉”）。



The period of awakening and high hopes was eventually followed by historical processes familiar from the history of religions: **dogmatic rigidity and progressive institutionalization led to a loss of immediacy in the original promise of salvation.** In the face of the disappointments in the wake of the French Revolution, enlightened hopes for an emancipation of humanity as a whole from the bonds of immaturity and irrationality began to wane. Hopes for an overarching integration of knowledge from an emancipatory perspective still survived in such philosophical programs as the natural philosophy of the so-called German idealism associated with Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel, Friedrich Hölderlin, and Friedrich Schelling. One of their original questions was about the implications of the existence of moral beings for the constitution of the physical world: what must a world look like in which moral beings with the freedom to think and act are possible at all? Today, the Anthropocene brings us back in dramatic ways to such a crossroad of natural and moral philosophy.

在觉醒和满怀希望的时期之后，最终出现了宗教史上所熟悉的历史进程：**教条主义的僵化和逐步制度化导致最初的救赎承诺失去了直接性。**面对法国大革命后的种种失望，人们对整个人类摆脱不成熟和非理性束缚的启蒙希望开始减弱。从解放的角度来看，对知识进行全面整合的希望仍存在于哲学过程之中，如与格奥尔格-威廉-弗里德里希-黑格尔、弗里德里希-荷尔德林和弗里德里希-谢林有关的所谓德国唯心主义的自然哲学。他们最初提出的问题之一是道德存在对物质世界构成的影响：一个拥有思想和行为自由的道德存在的世界应该是什么样的？今天，“人类世 ”以戏剧性的方式将我们带回了自然哲学和道德哲学的十字路口。



As discussed in [chapter 10](#743285), however, science and technology of the nineteenth century began to evolve much more rapidly than philosophers could anticipate or incorporate into their frameworks. This expansion led to a different type of science, namely, what one might call “science as a church” owing to the institutionalization of scientific practices and the strict epistemic norms imposed on it. From the perspective of the individual, the belief in science as a church was to a large degree determined by the increasing dominance of organizational structures, procedures, and hierarchies at universities, academies, and research organizations—a dominance that could hardly be questioned. The original promise of salvation—which, at least in principle, was still made to all of humanity—was thus ultimately diluted to an institutional mission celebrated on ceremonial occasions in praise of the unity of science.

然而，正如[第10章](#743285)所讨论的那样，19世纪科学技术的发展速度远远超过了哲学家们的预期或将其纳入其框架的速度。这种扩展导致了一种不同类型的科学，即由于科学实践的制度化和强加给它的严格的认识论规范，人们可以称之为 “作为教会的科学”。从个人的角度来看，科学作为教堂的信念在很大程度上是由大学、学术机构和研究组织的组织结构、程序和等级制度日益增长的主导地位决定的——这种主导地位几乎不容置疑。因此，最初的救赎承诺——至少在原则上仍是对全人类的承诺——最终被淡化为一种机构使命，在赞美科学统一性的仪式场合加以庆祝。



The awareness that scientific unity not only entails a unity of method or epistemology but also an actual integration of content, fragmented as it may be over a variety of disciplines, was nevertheless preserved in certain scientific traditions, such as in those of geography and evolutionary biology, as the works of Alexander von Humboldt and Charles Darwin make clear. In the five volumes of his famous *Cosmos*, Humboldt tried to give an overview of contemporary scientific knowledge, showing “nature as one great whole, moved and animated by internal forces.” As discussed in [chapter 7](#483065), Darwin integrated knowledge from areas as diverse as morphology and geography. The tradition of knowledge integration was preserved in later efforts as well. Prominent examples from the nineteenth century include Hermann von Helmholtz, Henri Poincaré, and Ernst Mach.

科学的统一性不仅意味着方法论或认识论的统一，而且意味着内容的整合，尽管这种整合可能分散在不同的学科中，但在某些科学传统中，如在地理学和生物进化论传统中，仍保留着这种意识，亚历山大-冯-洪堡和查尔斯-达尔文的著作就清楚地表明了这一点。洪堡在其著名的《宇宙》（*Cosmos*）的五卷本中，试图概述当代科学知识，展示 “大自然是一个巨大的整体，由内部力量推动和激活”。正如[第 7 章](#483065)所述，达尔文整合了形态学和地理学等不同领域的知识。知识整合的传统在后来的工作中也得到了保留。十九世纪的杰出代表包括赫尔曼-冯-亥姆霍兹（Hermann von Helmholtz）、亨利-庞加莱（Henri Poincaré）和恩斯特-马赫（Ernst Mach）。



An integrative outlook on science and its role in society was also encouraged by its popularization. An outstanding example of the influence these popular notions might have on an individual is personified by the young Albert Einstein, who devoured Aaron Bernstein’s *Naturwissenschaftliche Volksbücher.* These small volumes popularized the recent advancements of science with an emphasis on its international, cooperative character; its affinity with social and political progress; and a critical attitude with regard to authorities. They gave the young Einstein an understanding of science as a human undertaking that he could not only admire but in which he could also actively participate himself—with well-known consequences not only for his scientific achievements but also for his views on the moral and political implications of science. Toward the end of his life, Einstein still remembered the impact that his reading of popular science books had in overcoming his prior religious beliefs and more generally on his outlook on the world: “Mistrust of every kind of authority grew out of this experience, a skeptical attitude toward the convictions that were alive in any specific social environment—an attitude that has never again left me, even though, later on, it has been tempered by a better insight into the causal connections.” **Einstein’s example underlines the significance of both a scientific worldview and its alliance with reflective and critical thinking about the world in all of its dimensions.** As Bonneuil and Fressoz write: “To strive for decent lives in the Anthropocene therefore means freeing ourselves from repressive institutions, from alienating dominations and imaginaries. It can be an extraordinary emacipatory experience.”

科学及其在社会中的作用的综合观点也受到了科学普及的鼓励。年轻的阿尔伯特-爱因斯坦（Albert Einstein）就是这些大众观念可能对个人产生影响的一个突出例子，他阅读了亚伦-伯恩斯坦（Aaron Bernstein）的《自然科学大众读本》（Naturwissenschaftliche Volksbücher ）。这些书让年轻的爱因斯坦了解到，科学是一项人类事业，他不仅可以钦佩科学，还可以积极参与其中——这不仅对他的科学成就，也对他对科学的道德和政治影响的看法产生了众所周知的影响。在生命的最后阶段，爱因斯坦仍然记得阅读科普书籍对他克服之前的宗教信仰以及对他的世界观所产生的影响：“对各种权威的不信任源于这种经历，一种对任何特定社会环境中存在的信念的怀疑态度——这种态度再也没有离开过我，尽管后来我对因果联系有了更好的洞察力，使这种态度有所收敛”。**爱因斯坦的例子强调了科学世界观及其与反思性和批判性思维相结合的重要性。**正如 Bonneuil 和 Fressoz 所写的那样： 因此，在 “人类世 ”中争取体面的生活意味着将我们自己从压制性的制度、异化的统治和想象中解放出来。这可能是一种非凡的激动人心的体验"。

To conclude, the very possibility of addressing our current global challenges continues to depend on processes of knowledge integration based on globally shared experiences and mediated by critical reflection that includes the societal conditions for the evolution of knowledge as well as its eschatological dimension. But we must maintain a careful balance, aware of the temptations, pitfalls, and transgressions of which the history of science reminds us.

总之，应对当前全球性挑战的可能性仍然取决于以全球共享经验为基础、以批判性思考为媒介的知识整合进程，其中包括知识演变的社会条件及其末世论层面。但我们必须保持谨慎的平衡，意识到科学史提醒我们的诱惑、陷阱和过失。



On the one hand, in confronting the Anthropocene, we should reorient the current knowledge economy toward global responsibility. We should integrate local perspectives and find new ways of combining problem-oriented research with teaching and learning inside and outside of academia. Civic engagement and courage will also be imperative in the unavoidable global transformation processes that will follow. This is a complex challenge that requires the careful identification of cognitive and institutional structures capable of sustaining such a reorientation. The transformation will have to include the development of new curricula and research agendas, the interlocking of multiple knowledge dimensions, and critical engagement with the entanglement of knowledge with political, economic, and moral issues—these are the hallmarks of science in the twenty-first century. **Societies require mechanisms and institutions to help them ensure that their policies use and comply with the available knowledge on global challenges.**

一方面，在面对 “人类世 ”的过程中，我们应该调整当前知识经济的方向，使其承担起全球责任。我们应结合本地视角，找到新的方法，将以问题为导向的研究与学术界内外的教学结合起来。在接下来不可避免的全球转型过程中，公民的参与和勇气也将势在必行。这是一个复杂的挑战，需要认真确定能够维持这种调整的认知和体制结构。转型必须包括制定新的课程和研究议程，将多个知识层面交织在一起，以及批判性地处理知识与政治、经济和道德问题之间的纠葛——这些都是二十一世纪科学的标志。**社会需要各种机制和机构来帮助他们确保其政策使用并符合有关全球挑战的现有知识。**



On the other hand, given the long-term innovation cycles of science and its quintessential need for autonomy, it remains necessary to muster societal support for high-trust exploratory research free of utilitarian constraints, while maintaining the methodological standards and the stability of knowledge traditionally offered by the disciplines. The new scientific knowledge needed to address the global challenges of the Anthropocene can only be generated if the freedom of science to self-organize (which is at the roots of its innovative potential) is strengthened rather than weakened through further layers of control or short-term expectations; if problem choice in science can be fostered by reflection and trust, instead of being enforced by external pressure or formalized career patterns; if the necessary reality checks of intellectual ventures are not used as a pretext for the confinement of science to a blind race for “progress” or economically profitable applications; if the social and institutional structures of science encourage intellectual mobility and recruitment from all strata of a global society; and if new ways of accessing scientific information are not blocked by its transformation into a commodity.

另一方面，考虑到科学的长期创新周期及其对自主性的本质需求，仍然有必要争取社会对不受功利限制的高信任度探索性研究的支持，同时保持传统学科所提供的方法标准和知识的稳定性。要应对人类世的全球性挑战，就必须生成新的科学知识。而要做到这一点，就必须强化而非削弱科学自我组织的自由（这是其创新潜力的根源），不能通过更多的控制或短期预期来实现；必须通过反思和信任来促进科学中的问题选择，而不是通过外部压力或形式化的职业模式来强制；必须避免将对智力探索的必要现实检验作为将科学限制在盲目追求“进步”或经济上可盈利的应用的借口；必须使科学的社会和制度结构鼓励来自全球社会各阶层的知识流动和人才招募；还必须避免将获取科学信息的新途径因科学信息被转化为商品而受阻。



Some of the knowledge that remains in the dark can, however, only be generated, shared, and implemented by direct participation in the struggle to change the human condition—that is, in practical attempts and local initiatives to find concrete responses to the problems on the ground with which the Anthropocene confronts us. Whatever way we encounter these problems as individuals, realizing that science is part of a global, comprehensive evolution of knowledge (and not just an elitist pursuit for and by experts) may help to restore the life-orienting dimension that was a hallmark of early modern science and its emancipatory legacy. Thereby, in searching for that elusive, shadowy knowledge, scientists may again become collaborators in a workshop of hopes, including humanity’s hope for survival.

然而，有些仍处于未知领域的知识，唯有通过直接参与改变人类生存状况的斗争才能产生、分享和实施——也就是说，通过在实践中尝试和在地方上发起行动，来寻找切实可行的办法应对人类世带给我们的现实问题。无论我们作为个体以何种方式遭遇这些问题，认识到科学是全球性、全面性知识演进的一部分（而不仅仅是专家们为专家们所进行的精英式追求），或许有助于恢复早期现代科学及其解放遗产所具有的那种以生命为导向的维度。如此一来，在探寻那难以捉摸、隐秘的知识时，科学家们或许又能成为“希望的工作坊”中的合作者，其中包括人类生存的希望。
