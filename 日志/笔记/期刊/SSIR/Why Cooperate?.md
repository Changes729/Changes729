**01.1 patterns of social relations**

> In other words, we need to go beyond the observation that **patterns of social relations matter for cooperation**, to study the mechanisms through which social relations may enhance collective outcomes.
>
> 换句话说，我们需要超越以下观察，即社会关系模式对合作而言重要，以研究社会关系可以增强集体成果的机制。

**01.2 repeated interaction**

> repeated interaction makes it convenient for individuals to cooperate because long-term gains will offset the benefit of one-shot free riding.
>
> 重复的互动使得个体合作更加方便，因为长期的收益会抵消一次性搭便车的好处。

**01.3 network centrality and nonredundant social ties**

> First, relying on detailed social network information, I assess the relationship between social network position and collective outcomes, showing that farmers with greater network centrality and nonredundant social ties are more cooperative and participate more in the life of their producer organization.
>
> 首先，依靠详细的社交网络信息，我评估了社交网络地位和集体成果之间的关系，这表明具有更大的网络中心性和非冗余社会关系的农民更加合作，并且更多地参与了生产者组织的生活。

- Network centrality（网络中心性）

  在社会网络分析（SNA, Social Network Analysis）中，中心性是衡量一个人在网络中“有多核心”的指标。

  如果一个人和很多人有直接联系，而且这些人又是网络中重要的节点，那么他的位置就比较“中心”。

  - **Degree centrality**（度中心性）：直接连接的数量多，说明信息和资源能更快地流到你这里。
  - **Betweenness centrality**（中介中心性）：你处在很多人之间的“桥梁”位置，能控制信息流动的路径。
  - **Closeness centrality**（接近中心性）：你到网络中其他所有人的路径都很短，意味着你获取或传递信息很高效。

- Nonredundant social ties（非冗余社会联系）

  在社会网络理论（尤其是 Ronald Burt 的 *structural holes* 理论）中，如果两个联系人之间没有直接联系，你与他们各自的关系就是**非冗余的**。

  - **冗余联系**：如果A和B已经紧密联系，那么你同时认识他们并不会带来额外的新信息或新资源，因为他们互相之间已经交流过。

    **非冗余联系**：你认识的两个对象彼此不认识，这样你就能从两个不同的圈子获得**不重复的信息和机会**。

    非冗余联系能让你跨越“结构洞”（structural holes），把不同小圈子的资源和信息连接起来。

**01.4 Behavioral Games（BGs）**

> Motives and expectations are not immediately observable. However, it is possible to infer them from the way people behave in controlled, experimental settings, and in behavioral games ðBGsÞ in particular. BGs are abstract situations in which individuals have to allocate resources between themselves and other players. BGs are uniquely suited to capture actors’ interdependence in decision making, because in order to define their own strategies, participants must take into account their expectations concerning the behavior of other players.
>
> 动机和期望无法立即观察到。但是，可以从人们在受控的，实验设置和行为游戏中的行为方式兼而有之推断出它们。 BG是抽象情况，个人必须在自己和其他玩家之间分配资源。 BG非常适合捕获演员在决策中的相互依存关系，因为为了定义自己的策略，参与者必须考虑到他们对其他参与者行为的期望。
>
> Despite the scarce use of BGs in sociology ðwhich, I believe, is partly due to their initial association with economics and rational choice theoryÞ, in recent years there has been an interesting evolution in the use of BGs that has made them a promising tool for the social sciences, and sociology in particular.7 Initially, behavioral experiments were developed to reveal general patterns of human behavior ðMarwell and Ames 1979; Fehr and Gächter 2002; Camerer 2003Þ, using convenience samples ðoften college studentsÞ and experimental settings and protocols that guaranteed complete anonymity and, as much as possible, “stripped” participants of their background characteristics and experiences. Over the last two decades, however, some scholars have shifted their interest toward macrocultural variations and started to play BGs with diverse populations around the world ðHenrich et al. 2001, 2010; Herrmann et al. 2008; Yamagishi 2011Þ. Finally, in the last 10 years, BGs have started to be used to measure individual and group differences that stem from microcontextual variations as well as personal and group experiences ðBarr 2003; Carpenter, Daniere, and Takahashi 2004; Karlan 2005; Fearon, Humphreys, and Weinstein 2009; Ermisch and Gambetta 2010; Grossman and Baldassarri 2012; Baldassarri and Grossman 2013Þ. This last development is critical: that BGs have been shown to be sufficiently sensitive to detect differences between individuals within a society makes them an exceptionally powerful tool for research in those fields of sociology that rely on “hard-to-measure” concepts such as trust, authority, altruism, reciprocity, and solidarity, among others.
>
> 我认为，尽管BG在社会学中缺乏使用，部分原因是它们与经济学和理性选择理论的最初相关，但近年来，BGS的使用中有一个有趣的发展，使它们成为社会科学的有前途的工具，尤其是社会学，尤其是行为实验，以揭示了人类行为的一般行为; Fehr andGächter2002; Camerer2003Þ，使用便利样本兼说大学的学生以及实验设置和协议，以确保完全匿名，并尽可能多地“剥夺”其背景特征和经验的参与者。然而，在过去的二十年中，一些学者将他们的兴趣转移到了宏观文化的变化上，并开始在世界各地的人群中扮演BGS。 2001年，2010年； Herrmann等。 2008; Yamagishi2011Þ。最后，在过去的十年中，BGS已开始用于衡量源于微敏感变化以及个人和小组经验的个人和群体差异ðBarr2003; Carpenter，Daniere和Takahashi 2004； Karlan 2005; Fearon，Humphreys和Weinstein 2009; Ermisch和Gambetta 2010； Grossman和Baldassarri 2012； Baldassarri和Grossman2013Þ。最后的发展至关重要：已证明BG足够敏感以检测社会中个人之间的差异，使其成为依赖“难以估量”概念的社会学领域的极有力的工具，例如信任，权威，利他主义，互惠，互惠和团结等等。



**01.5 Dictator game(DG)**

> Traditionally, in a classic DG, two subjects are given a common endowment. One of the players, randomly chosen, has to decide how to divide the money between himself or herself and the other player, the receiver. Deciders keep to themselves whatever they have decided to allocate to themselves, while the receivers take home whatever they have been given. The DG is conducted under conditions of anonymity. If deciders were completely selfish, they would keep the entire endowment to themselves. In contrast, individuals share, on average, between 20% and 30% of their endowment. A few share up to half, whereas the modal behavior is to give nothing. Behavior in a DG is usually interpreted as an expression of otherregarding preferences ðor inequality aversionÞ and used to classify individuals as selfish or altruistic types ðCamerer 2003; Barr, Lindelow, and Sernees 2009Þ.8
>
> 传统上，在经典的DG中，有两个主题是一个共同的捐赠。一位随机选择的球员必须决定如何将自己或其他球员之间的钱分配给接球手。决定自己决定分配自己的任何东西，而接收者将他们带回家。 DG是在匿名条件下进行的。如果决定者完全是自私的，他们将保留自己的全部赋。相比之下，个人平均共享其end赋的20％至30％。一些分享到一半，而模态行为是什么都不给。 DG中的行为通常被解释为其他偏爱的表达ð或不平等的厌恶，并用来将个体归类为自私或利他类型ðCamerer2003； Barr，Lindelow和Sernees2009Þ.8
